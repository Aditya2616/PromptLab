# PromptLab research notes

## 1. The field

| Method | Idea | Take-away for PromptLab |
|---|---|---|
| APE (Zhou et al. 2022, [2211.01910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01910)) | LLM proposes instructions from input-output demos, score on held-out samples, keep best, optional resample around the best. | Baseline "generate then pick best" and the idea of scoring on a sample, not the full set. |
| OPRO (Yang et al. 2023, [2309.03409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03409)) | The optimiser LLM sees a history of (prompt, score) pairs and proposes a better one. | Needs a strong optimiser LLM (PaLM 2-L, GPT-4). Not reliable with a 0.5B model. |
| EvoPrompt (Guo et al. 2023, [2309.08532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532)) | Genetic algorithm / differential evolution where crossover and mutation are done by an LLM; population scored on a dev set. | Direct blueprint for the population loop, selection and crossover. |
| PromptBreeder (Fernando et al. 2023, [2309.16797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16797)) | Evolves task prompts and the mutation prompts themselves; many mutation operator families. | Inspiration for the operator catalogue and for tracking which operator produced each win. Self-referential mutation is out of scope. |
| MIPROv2 (Opsahl-Ong et al. 2024, [2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11695), DSPy) | Bayesian search over instruction candidates and few-shot demo sets. | Joint search over instruction and demonstrations matches my few-shot add/remove operators. |
| TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al. 2024, [2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)) | LLM-written textual "gradients" feed back into a prompt. | Needs a capable critic. Not viable on a 0.5B model; documented as out of scope. |
| GEPA (Agrawal et al. 2025, [2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)) | Reflective mutation from failure traces plus a Pareto front over per-example scores so diverse candidates survive. | Two ideas adopted: show failures to the mutator, and optional Pareto-aware parent selection (per-example score vectors are cheap with NumPy). The authors' numbers are their own; v1 and v2 of the paper report different averages. |

Related: a grammar-guided evolutionary paper ([2507.10326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10326)) reports gains on small LLMs and notes they are very sensitive to prompt wording; a toolbox paper on evolutionary prompt search ([2511.05120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05120)). I read these only via search summaries, not in full.

### What works with small models
- I found no controlled head-to-head of all methods on sub-1B models; most headline numbers are on large models. I will not claim otherwise.
- Small models are extremely format-sensitive. My own probe (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, 48 CommonsenseQA validation items, greedy): "Answer the question. Reply with only the letter." gave 54.2%, "You are a helpful assistant." gave 12.5%. Much of the achievable gain is therefore output-format control. The report must say so.
- A 0.5B model is a weak rewriter. LLM rewrites will often be degenerate, so the mutator is guarded (length limit, must differ, non-empty, deduplicated) and cheap deterministic operators carry real weight.
- Dev sets of 100-200 items are noisy (SE about 3.5 points at 50% accuracy). Selection on them overfits, so the final report uses a separate test split, bootstrap CIs and a paired significance test.

## 2. Practical constraints measured here
- 12 logical cores, CPU torch 2.11, transformers 5.7, numpy 2.4, openai 2.33. `datasets` is not installed; data is fetched as parquet with `huggingface_hub` and read with pandas.
- Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (fp32, batch 16, 6 new tokens): about 0.22 s per example (about 11 s per 48 items). A 150-item dev evaluation is about 35 s per uncached variant; a 6-generation run with 8 children per generation is roughly 30 min. The cache makes reruns near-instant.

## 5. Statistics
- Bootstrap CI of accuracy and of the paired difference (best minus seed) on the test split, 10,000 resamples, NumPy.
- Exact McNemar test (binomial on discordant pairs), plus a paired bootstrap p-value.
- Selection-bias display: dev score of the winner vs its test score (winner's curse gap).
- Control: random search at equal evaluation budget (same operators, no selection pressure), so the evolutionary claim is tested against a baseline and not only against the seed.
